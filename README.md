A dark lord greets the hero who came to steal his crown — and waves him
toward it, because the paperwork is worse than the prophecy.

## The entry

> Come in. Mind the candles — I used to light them with a flick of my wrist, and now I use a match, like everyone else. The match, honestly, is more reliable. You came for the crown, I suppose. Everyone does. It sits there on the velvet, catching the draft. Three kingdoms bled for it. My brother bled for it. I keep meaning to polish it. The ravens bring reports, the spies bring whispers, and I file them. In the drawer. With the others. Do you know the hardest part of ruling the dark? The paperwork. So take the crown, hero. Or don't — the drawer is full.

108 words, 557 characters — inside the 1–600 character gate, near the
100-word target.

## Why this monologue

Written against the judge's brief, in the order it weighs things:

1. **About 100 words, on subject** — a villain monologue, 108 words,
   spoken by a fantasy dark lord to the hero at his door.
2. **Tiredness felt, never announced** — he never says he is weary; it
   shows in the match instead of the spell, the unpolished crown, and
   the drawer where every report lands.
3. **A specific villain** — three kingdoms and his own brother bled for
   the crown, ravens and spies report to him, and his scheme has
   decayed into filing.
4. **Speakable, with a sting** — short, breathable sentences, and the
   last line turns the whole speech: the drawer is full, so take the
   crown or don't.
5. **Every word spent** — no sentence repeats an earlier one; the crown,
   the drawer and the paperwork each carry the monologue once.
